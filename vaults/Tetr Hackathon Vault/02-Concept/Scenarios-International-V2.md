## 📞 1. COLD CALL — "The Gym Owner"
*(broader-appeal target: neighborhood gym, not gender-coded)*

**Product you're selling**: **Clara** — an AI assistant for small businesses. Monitors Instagram / WhatsApp / Google DMs, auto-replies to FAQs, books appointments, captures leads. $89/mo. 10-min phone setup. No tech skills needed.

### Setup
**You**: 24, 5 months at Clara (YC-backed AI startup). First job out of university. 60 cold calls a day — 95% don't pick up, 4% say no in 10 seconds, 1% becomes a demo. Manager hinted you're near PIP.

**Them**: **Marcus Reed**, 38, owner of **Reed Strength & Conditioning** — a neighborhood gym. Built it solo over 6 years, now has 2 part-time coaches. 3 weeks ago a TikTok of his 60-year-old client deadlifting 200lbs hit 1.2M views. Instagram DMs went from 15/day to 300/day. He's replied to roughly a quarter of them. *Knows* he's losing leads and new-member sign-ups. Burned by a bad chatbot a year ago that kept telling members his gym was closed — he hates the word "AI."

**How you got here**: You scraped his gym from a list of viral small-biz accounts. Phone number public on his Instagram bio. Called twice this morning — he answered because he was expecting a delivery.

**The moment**: *"Yeah?"* — distracted, walking the floor between sets.

### Palette (6 buttons)
1. **Pattern Interrupt** — break the cold-call script upfront
2. **Accusation Audit** — pre-empt her "this is a salesperson" defense
3. **Labeling** — name her DM chaos before she does
4. **Calibrated Question** — "how are you handling the flood right now?"
5. **No-Oriented Question** — "would it be crazy to show you 15 min tomorrow?"
6. **Soft Close** — ask for the next step, not the sale

### Winning path (3★)
Pattern Interrupt → Accusation Audit → Label her situation → One Calibrated Question → Soft Close for tomorrow.
Never pitch product. Never say "AI." Never use "how are you today?".

### Twist
At minute 2 she says *"Just email me."* 94% of those = dead. You have ONE line to redirect. (Correct play: a tiny bit of push-back + No-Oriented Question: *"Would it be insane if I stole 5 minutes tomorrow instead? If what I show is off, you email-graveyard me forever and I'll respect it."*)

### Lesson Card
> **Today's technique: Pattern Interrupt + Labeling**
>
> In the first 3 seconds of a cold call, the other person's brain screams "salesperson → hang up." You cannot win from inside that pattern. Break it.
>
> Don't try to sound friendly. Don't "hey how are you today!" — she knows. Instead, tell the truth: *"You don't know me. This is a cold call. Can I have 30 seconds, then you decide?"* That's the Pattern Interrupt. 80% of the time, you now have her attention.
>
> Then: **Labeling**. Before she describes her pain, you name it for her: *"Sounds like your DMs have completely gotten away from you."* When she hears her own reality coming out of a stranger's mouth, she leans in. That's how trust starts on a cold call.
>
> The full flow: **Interrupt → Label → Ask → Small Ask**. Never sell the product on call #1. Sell the next 15 minutes of her time.

## 💼 2. INTERVIEW OPENER — "The Senior Role You Don't Quite Deserve Yet"

### Setup
**You**: 3.5 yrs as a PM II at a mid-size SaaS. Recruited for a **Senior PM** role at a YC-backed European startup. Thin on paper, strong in substance — your last feature drove $1.2M in upsell.

**Them**: **Dan**, VP Product, 41. Just flew in from Amsterdam. 8 interviews this week. Will skim LinkedIn mid-answer if you're generic.

**How you got here**: Passed recruiter screen. Hiring-manager gate. 10 others in the pipeline.

**The moment**: He sips coffee. *"So. Tell me about yourself."*

### Palette (5 buttons)
1. **Past–Present–Future** — the core frame for this question
2. **The Hook** — a single memorable line in sentence 2 that makes him put the phone down
3. **Callback** — reference something from the JD (proves you researched)
4. **STAR Snippet** — one micro-story to back up one claim
5. **Soft Close** — offer a bridge into the next question

### Winning path (3★)
Past–Present–Future structure, Hook in sentence 2, one STAR-backed claim, end with a question that hands him control.

### Twist
At minute 4 he'll pretend to take a phone call mid-answer. Test = do you panic/re-start/apologize, or do you hold your ground and pick up exactly where you stopped?

### Lesson Card
> **Today's technique: Past–Present–Future + The Hook**
>
> "Tell me about yourself" is not an autobiography request. It's a 90-second ad. Structure = Past (where you were) → Present (what you own now) → Future (why you're here).
>
> But structure alone is forgettable. You need **The Hook** — one line in your first 20 seconds that makes the interviewer stop doing what they were doing. A specific number, a surprising fact, a contrast. Not "I'm a PM with 3 years of experience." Instead: *"I shipped one feature last year that drove $1.2M in upsell — and that's the story that convinced me I'm ready for the senior scope here."*
>
> The Hook earns you the next 80 seconds of attention. Without it, you're background noise.

## 💰 3. SALARY NEGOTIATION — "The Offer You Thought Was Done"

### Setup
**You**: Just got verbal *"we want to make you an offer"* after 5 rounds. Hoping for $85k base. Company said *"before paperwork — align on comp."*

**Them**: **Diana**, HR BP, 38. Friendly, dog pics in her signature. Band: $72k–$108k. Her bonus depends on closing below midpoint.

**How you got here**: You let slip in round 2 that you make $72k. She anchored.

**The moment**: *"Great, so — what are you thinking for base?"*

### Palette (6 buttons)
1. **Anchoring** — establish your number with justification
2. **Tactical Silence** — say nothing after a number
3. **Calibrated Question** — "what would it take to get to $X?"
4. **BATNA Reveal** — mention the other opportunity (real, not bluffed)
5. **Higher Authority** — "let me think overnight + check with my partner"
6. **Named Fake Objection** — call out "that's stretching the band" when it isn't

### Winning path (3★)
Anchor at $95k with market justification → Silence after → Calibrated Question when she pushes back → Name "stretching the band" as what it is → close at ≥$92k + signing bonus.

### Twist
She'll say *"I can do $82k today, that's already stretching the band"* as if it's final. It's not. The band top is $108k. Her job is to close you cheap.

### Lesson Card
> **Today's technique: Anchoring + Silence**
>
> Whoever says the first number, wins — IF the number is credible and justified. "$95k, based on what I'm seeing in market for this scope at companies your size" is credible. "$120k because I'm great" is not.
>
> Then — **shut up**. After you've said your number, count to 4. Out loud if you have to. The counterpart will almost always fill the silence, usually by improving their position. Breaking your own silence is the #1 self-inflicted wound in salary negotiations.
>
> Bonus: if they say "that's stretching the band" — it almost never is. Name it politely: *"That's helpful context — what would it take to get to $95k?"* You just pivoted from negotiating against their floor to negotiating toward your ceiling.

## 🎯 4. MARKETING PRICING — "The Client Who Wants Everything Cheap"

### Setup
**You**: Freelance marketer, 2 years solo. You quoted a $2,500/mo retainer (content + social + paid ads) for a 4-person team. You're 6 days from rent due.

**Them**: **Lauren Kim**, 34, founder of a DTC skincare brand growing fast but not yet rich. Smart, warm. Talked to 5 marketers who quoted lower. She likes you. Has budget for $1,700 but won't admit it.

**How you got here**: Her email: *"Your proposal looks great! Quick call?"* You know what's coming.

**The moment**: *"I love everything you proposed but honestly the number is scaring me — can we make this work?"*

### Palette (5 buttons)
1. **Labeling** — name the fear before defending the price
2. **Value-Based Pricing** — reframe price against outcome
3. **Split vs Hold** — restructure scope, don't split the difference
4. **BATNA Reveal** — mention you have a waitlist (if true)
5. **Permission to Be Direct** — "can I be blunt for a second?"

### Winning path (3★) — any of three
(a) Hold $2,500 with outcome reframing
(b) Restructure scope cleanly to $1,700 (drop paid ads, keep content + social)
(c) Walk with dignity and the door open
**All three can be 3★**. What makes it 0★ is dropping price without cutting scope.

### Twist
At minute 3 she says *"the other marketers quoted me $1,200 — what am I missing?"* The trap: don't trash competitors. The answer: *"If they quoted $1,200 for 4 people's time, ask what you're actually getting. I'll be direct — at $2,500 you're getting X outputs and $Y in attributable revenue. At $1,200 you're getting a junior content writer."*

### Lesson Card
> **Today's technique: Split vs Hold on Price**
>
> The worst thing you can do in a pricing negotiation is meet in the middle. If you quote $2,500 and they counter $1,500, splitting at $2,000 = you just lost 20% of your price AND you just taught them you cave under pressure. Every future invoice will be haggled.
>
> Instead: **hold your rate, restructure the scope.** "I can't drop the rate, but I can drop the paid ads management — which brings it to $1,750. Want to see what that looks like?"
>
> You kept your rate. She kept her budget. The scope absorbed the difference. Your integrity is intact. And she *respects* you more, not less.

## 🏢 5. ENTERPRISE DISCOVERY — "The COO Only on the Call as a Favor"

### Setup
**You**: AE at **Atlas**, B2B ops SaaS. ACV $95k. Inherited from a rep who quit. Research thin. 30 minutes.

**Them**: **Alex Mercer**, 46, COO at **NorthWind Logistics** (800 people, Chicago HQ). Joined 9 months ago. Fired 2 vendors already. 3 half-overlapping tools up for renewal.

**How you got here**: His VP of Ops **Cristina** pushed him to take the call. He resents it.

**The moment**: Camera off. *"30 minutes. We're not looking. Cristina asked me to hear you out. Pitch me. Five minutes."*

### Palette (6 buttons)
1. **Accusation Audit** — defuse his resentment about being there
2. **SPIN** — Situation → Problem → Implication → Need-payoff, in order
3. **Teach** — show him something about his business he doesn't know
4. **Labeling** — "sounds like you've been burned by tools that overpromised"
5. **Named Fake Objection** — when he says "we already have [Competitor]"
6. **Soft Close** — invite Cristina to round 2

### Winning path (3★)
Accusation Audit first 20 sec ("I hear you — you're here as a favor, I'll earn the 30 minutes or let you go early"). Then refuse to pitch. Ask SPIN questions for 15 minutes. Teach him one thing he didn't know about his own stack. End with: "Would it be useful if I came back next week and Cristina joined?"

### Twist
Minute 10 he drops *"we already have [Competitor]"*. Read: is this true, or is it a test? (It's usually 50/50.) The right response is Labeling + Calibrated Question: *"Sounds like you're set with them — what would be true for you to consider looking anyway?"*

### Lesson Card
> **Today's technique: Discovery over Demo (SPIN)**
>
> The #1 mistake in enterprise sales is showing up to a discovery call and doing a product demo. You lose the room in 5 minutes.
>
> Instead: ask the 4-question arc.
> • **Situation**: "How are you handling X today?"
> • **Problem**: "Where does that break down?"
> • **Implication**: "What does that cost you in time / money / team morale?"
> • **Need-payoff**: "If that got solved, what would it be worth?"
>
> By question 4, **they're selling themselves**. Your job is to nod, take notes, and ask the next Implication question. The Implication question is where the budget gets unlocked — most reps skip it and wonder why deals stall.

## 🎭 6. CLOSING THE SALES COACH — "The Hardest Buyer You'll Ever Meet"

### Setup
**You**: AE selling a conversation-intelligence tool that records + scores sales calls. Warm lead — but notorious.

**Them**: **Paul Hargreaves**, 52, London sales-training consultant, 20 yrs ex-Oracle. Has taught 4,000+ reps every objection you're about to throw. Knows your product, knows the competitor's price. Will coach you in real-time if you use a tired line.

**How you got here**: He booked himself. SDR's note: *"Warning — Paul only takes demos to steal tactics."*

**The moment**: *"Alright, go. Why should I care? And please — no 'great question.'"*

### Palette (6 buttons)
1. **Permission to Be Direct** — "can I just cut the normal script?"
2. **Teach–Tailor–Take-Control (Challenger)** — teach him about his own training business
3. **Named Fake Objection** — call out his tests when they come
4. **Accusation Audit** — "I know you take demos to steal tactics, so let me just show you two things worth stealing"
5. **Calibrated Question** — "how would YOU sell this?"
6. **BATNA Reveal** — "I have 3 similar consultancies closing this quarter — what would make you one of them?"

### Winning path (3★)
Accusation Audit upfront → Teach him something about how his trainees' conversion rates correlate with talk/listen ratios → Name his first fake objection when it lands → Flip the dynamic by asking HIM how he'd sell it → Soft close on a pilot with 2 of his rep cohorts.

### Twist
Paul drops a deliberate, obviously-weak objection ("your pricing is too high") to see if you respond textbook (wrong — "let me justify") or *name it*: "*Paul, respectfully — that's not your real objection, you've already seen our pricing. What's the actual concern?*"

### Lesson Card
> **Today's technique: Naming the Fake Objection + The Challenger Sale**
>
> When a sophisticated buyer throws you an objection, ask: is this real? Sometimes it's a test. Sometimes it's autopilot. Sometimes it's a smokescreen.
>
> **Naming the fake objection** looks like this: *"I hear you say 'we already have a tool' — but my honest read is that's more a reflex than a no. What's the actual concern?"* It's confrontational, but it's also the biggest respect signal you can send a sophisticated buyer. It tells them you're not running a script.
>
> The Challenger Sale wraps around it: don't ask prospects what they want (they don't know). **Teach** them something about their business they didn't know. **Tailor** it to their exact world. **Take control** of the close. This works best with buyers smarter than you — which is most buyers worth having.

## 🎤 7. PITCH COMPETITION Q&A — "The Money Round"

### Setup
**You**: Co-founder of a climate-tech startup. Just delivered your 5-minute pitch. **$30,000 grant + top-tier accelerator slot** on the line. The pitch you rehearsed. The Q&A was always the weakness.

**Them**: 3 judges —
  • **Christian Weber** — seed-stage VC, will push TAM until it breaks
  • **Prof. Ishan Patel** — academic, disagrees with your technical approach
  • **Andrea Marchetti** — finance partner, wants unit economics

**How you got here**: Two teams already pitched. Both got shredded. One walked off in tears.

**The moment**: Christian: *"Your slide said 'multi-billion dollar market.' Walk me through how you got to that number."*

### Palette (5 buttons)
1. **Acknowledge → Reframe → Answer → Bridge** — the 4-step formula
2. **Bottom-Up Math** — specific numbers, not TAM platitudes
3. **Concede Small / Win Big** — accept one flaw, defend the thesis
4. **Permission to Disagree** — on Prof Patel's technical pushback
5. **Tactical Silence** — pause after finishing an answer (don't over-talk)

### Winning path (3★)
Acknowledge the TAM concern ("fair — most decks overclaim") → Reframe ("let me give you the bottom-up") → Answer with specific math → Bridge to your go-to-market focus. Concede Prof Patel's technical point gracefully while defending the thesis. Answer Andrea's unit economics crisply. Never over-talk after an answer.

### Twist
Prof Patel will interrupt Christian mid-answer with a completely different question. You must pick: finish your current answer, or pivot. Correct play: finish the Christian answer in one sentence, then pivot. Show you can hold a thread.

### Lesson Card
> **Today's technique: Acknowledge → Reframe → Answer → Bridge**
>
> Under hostile questioning, amateurs defend. Pros use a 4-step formula.
>
> 1. **Acknowledge** the question's legitimacy: "Fair question — most decks overclaim TAM." You've signaled humility.
> 2. **Reframe** to the terms you can win on: "Let me give you the bottom-up."
> 3. **Answer** with concrete specifics: "8,000 qualifying companies × $12k ACV = $96M SAM."
> 4. **Bridge** back to your narrative: "And that's why our focus is on the 400 who use X — that's where our ICP concentrates."
>
> Four beats, 30 seconds, done. The judge feels heard, impressed, redirected. It works in press calls, board meetings, podcast interviews, and cocktail-party arguments.

## 🚨 8. PRESS DAMAGE CONTROL — "The 41-Minute Bug"

### Setup
**You**: Founder/CEO of **Cashly**, personal finance app, 85k users. Last night at 2am a deploy exposed partial transaction history between users for 41 minutes. CTO caught + patched + DM'd affected users. You think it's handled.

**Them**: **Nathan Walsh**, 29, TechCrunch Europe. Has one furious user on record. Has screenshots. Writes *"Fintech App Leaks Customer Data for HOURS"* if you give him nothing better.

**How you got here**: His email at 8:12am. You picked up from your car. 12 minutes before the office.

**The moment**: *"Can you confirm how many users were affected, and when you became aware?"*

### Palette (5 buttons)
1. **Concrete Timeline** — specific times and counts
2. **Don't Repeat the Wrong Number** — when he fact-checks with bad data
3. **The Bridge** — short factual answer → your controlled narrative
4. **Accusation Audit** — "I know how this can sound — let me just walk you through what actually happened"
5. **Ownership + Next Action** — what you did + what you're doing next

### Winning path (3★)
Lead with Accusation Audit → Concrete Timeline (2:04am deploy, 2:45am patch, 9am notification, 842 users) → Bridge to ownership + concrete next steps (third-party audit, public postmortem in 72h) → Never repeat his wrong number when it shows up → Offer him the postmortem first.

### Twist
At minute 4 he asks *"So it was ~3,000 users, correct?"* The wrong number. If you say *"no it wasn't 3,000, it was 842"* — congrats, "3,000" ends up in the lede. Correct response: *"Let me give you the actual number — 842 users, all notified personally by 9 AM."* Never, ever repeat his number.

### Lesson Card
> **Today's technique: Don't Repeat the Wrong Number + Concrete Timeline**
>
> Every media crisis has a moment where the reporter throws you a wrong fact to see if you bite. "So it was ~3,000 users, right?" or "So you took a day to patch it?" Biting the correction — "No, it wasn't 3,000, it was 842" — guarantees "3,000" ends up in the article. Journalists quote the first number they hear repeated.
>
> Correct move: state the right number without echoing the wrong one. *"Let me give you the actual number — 842 users, all notified personally by 9 AM."*
>
> And always lead with a **concrete timeline**: specific minutes, specific counts, specific actions. Vagueness reads as spin. Specificity reads as ownership. A CEO who can say "2:04 AM to 2:45 AM, 842 users affected, here's exactly what we did" is infinitely more credible than one who says "a short window affecting a small number of users."

## What the player walks away knowing (the "course")

After playing the 8 scenarios, a player — with zero prior sales/persuasion knowledge — knows:
1. How to open a cold call (Pattern Interrupt)
2. How to answer "tell me about yourself" in any context (Past–Present–Future)
3. How to negotiate a salary (Anchor + Silence + Calibrated Q)
4. How to defend a price without caving (Split vs Hold)
5. How to run a discovery call that doesn't feel like a pitch (SPIN)
6. How to handle the smartest, most skeptical buyers (Name the Fake Objection)
7. How to handle hostile Q&A (Acknowledge → Reframe → Answer → Bridge)
8. How to survive a media crisis (Concrete Timeline + Don't Repeat the Wrong Number)

That's a genuine persuasion education, wrapped in a 30-minute game. **This is the pitch to the judges.**
