# Persuasion Techniques Library

The canonical list of real-world techniques that power CLUTCH. Every **tactic button** in the game is one of these. Every **Lesson Card** explains one of these in plain language.

Sources: Chris Voss (*Never Split the Difference*), Cialdini (*Influence*), Neil Rackham (*SPIN Selling*), Matt Dixon (*The Challenger Sale*), Sandler, classic negotiation theory (Fisher & Ury), public speaking / PR standards.

Rule for the game: never use the author's name in-game. Use the plain-English technique name + a 1-sentence definition + an example line. Non-technical players learn by doing, not by citation.

---

## 🟦 LISTENING & EMPATHY

### 1. Labeling
**What it is**: Name the other person's emotion or situation before they do.
**Why it works**: Makes them feel seen. Defuses negativity in ~80% of cases.
**Example line**: *"It sounds like you're drowning in messages right now."*
**Common mistake**: Labeling with "I feel like..." — makes it about you, not them.

### 2. Mirroring
**What it is**: Repeat the last 1–3 words the other person said, as a question.
**Why it works**: Forces them to elaborate. Buys you thinking time. Signals deep listening.
**Example line**: *"Tight on budget?"*
**Common mistake**: Mirroring too long a phrase — sounds mocking.

### 3. Tactical Silence
**What it is**: Stop talking after a number or an ask. Count to 4.
**Why it works**: Humans hate silence. They'll fill it — often by improving their offer.
**Example**: They say "I can do $82k." You say nothing. They say "…okay, maybe $86k."
**Common mistake**: Breaking the silence yourself.

### 4. Calibrated Question
**What it is**: Open-ended "how" or "what" question that can't be answered yes/no.
**Why it works**: Gets them solving your problem for you, without confrontation.
**Example line**: *"How am I supposed to do that?"* · *"What would make this work for you?"*
**Common mistake**: Asking "why" — triggers defensiveness.

### 5. No-Oriented Question
**What it is**: Ask a question where "no" is a safe answer that actually moves things forward.
**Why it works**: "No" feels like protection; "yes" feels like commitment. People prefer "no."
**Example line**: *"Would it be crazy to grab 15 minutes tomorrow?"* · *"Have you given up on fixing this?"*
**Common mistake**: Asking "would you be open to..." — classic desperate-salesperson giveaway.

### 6. Accusation Audit
**What it is**: Preempt their worst thoughts about you, out loud.
**Why it works**: Neutralizes objections before they harden.
**Example line**: *"You're going to think this is another generic cold call, and honestly it might be — can I have 30 seconds and you decide?"*
**Common mistake**: Over-selling the audit into a real confession.

## 🟧 FRAMING & POSITIONING

### 7. Pattern Interrupt
**What it is**: Break the expected script in the first 3 seconds.
**Why it works**: Prospects pattern-match "salesperson → dismiss" instantly. Break the pattern = earn 30 seconds of real attention.
**Example line** (cold call): *"Hi — you don't know me, this is a cold call, can I have 30 seconds before you hang up?"*
**Common mistake**: Pattern-interrupt that's too cute ("Hi I know you're not expecting me…") — they've heard it.

### 8. Anchoring
**What it is**: Throw out the first credible number. Sets the conversation range.
**Why it works**: The counterpart's brain now orbits your anchor, not their intended floor.
**Example line** (salary): *"Based on market for this scope, I'm looking at $95k to $110k."*
**Common mistake**: Anchoring without justification. Unjustified = loss of credibility.

### 9. Past–Present–Future Frame
**What it is**: Structure "tell me about yourself" or any story as: where you were → where you are → where you're going.
**Why it works**: Natural narrative arc. Easy to follow, hard to forget.
**Example line**: *"3 years ago I was managing 2 products at a 20-person SaaS. Today I own the roadmap for a $12M ARR line. Next, I want to scale a team of 6 PMs — which is why I'm here."*
**Common mistake**: Going full chronological ("I was born in…").

### 10. STAR Method
**What it is**: Situation → Task → Action → Result. For behavioral interview answers.
**Why it works**: Forces concrete specifics. Prevents rambling.
**Example line**: *"Last year our churn spiked 3 points [S]. I was asked to diagnose [T]. I ran a 12-user cancellation-call study [A]. We identified the bad onboarding flow and cut churn by 40% [R]."*
**Common mistake**: Skipping the Result — the whole story exists for the result.

### 11. Value-Based Pricing
**What it is**: Tie your price to the outcome you generate, not your cost or hours.
**Why it works**: "$2,500/mo" is abstract. "$2,500/mo for an extra $18k/mo in attributed revenue" is a no-brainer.
**Example line**: *"For a business your size, the last 3 clients saw $X in 90 days. The price of this program is framed against that outcome."*
**Common mistake**: Defending the hours behind the price ("it takes me 40 hours"). Nobody cares about your hours.

### 12. Reframe
**What it is**: Accept the question then change the terms of the debate.
**Why it works**: Lets you answer without conceding the premise.
**Example line**: *"That's a great question about cost — the bigger question is actually cost of inaction. Let me show you what that looks like."*
**Common mistake**: Reframing without acknowledging the original question. Feels slippery.

### 13. The Bridge
**What it is**: Answer briefly, then pivot to your key message.
**Why it works**: Press, interviews, Q&A — control the narrative without seeming evasive.
**Example line**: *"What I can tell you is [minimum fact]. What matters more is [your controlled message]."*
**Common mistake**: Over-bridging (i.e., not answering the actual question). Audience notices.

## 🟥 CLOSING & COMMITMENT

### 14. The Soft Close / Next-Step Ask
**What it is**: Never close the deal. Close the *next step*.
**Why it works**: Reduces commitment friction. "15 minutes on Thursday" is easier to say yes to than "$95k/year."
**Example line**: *"Would 15 minutes Thursday afternoon work, or is Friday better?"*
**Common mistake**: Asking "does that sound good?" — invites a "let me think about it."

### 15. Assumptive Close
**What it is**: Speak as if the deal is done, move to logistics.
**Why it works**: Lowers their psychological barrier to agreement.
**Example line**: *"I'll send an invite for Thursday 2pm — should I include your VP of Ops?"*
**Common mistake**: Assuming too early, before rapport.

### 16. Alternative Close
**What it is**: Give them two yes-paths, no no-path.
**Why it works**: Feels like choice; is actually funneling.
**Example line**: *"Do you want to start with the pilot, or the full rollout?"*
**Common mistake**: Forcing when they're not ready. Use only after clear buy-in.

### 17. "That's Right"
**What it is**: Summarize their situation so accurately they say "that's right" (not "you're right").
**Why it works**: "You're right" = they want to end the conversation. "That's right" = genuine agreement that shifts the frame to you.
**Example line**: After 3 labels: *"So it sounds like you've tried 2 tools, both missed the point, you're wary of hiring an employee, and you want something that works in under a week."* → *"That's right."*
**Common mistake**: Rushing to summary before earning it.

## 🟨 HANDLING RESISTANCE

### 18. BATNA Reveal
**What it is**: Mention your alternative (real or implied) without making it a threat.
**Why it works**: Shifts the power balance. They now know you can walk.
**Example line**: *"I've been talking to 2 other clients this week — I'm trying to figure out where I commit."*
**Common mistake**: Bluffing. They can smell it. Only reveal real BATNAs.

### 19. Split vs Hold on Price
**What it is**: When they counter your price, do NOT meet in the middle.
**Why it works**: Splitting the difference is the fastest way to lose on negotiation. Hold or restructure.
**Example line**: *"I can't drop the rate, but I can restructure the scope — which of these 3 deliverables matters least to you?"*
**Common mistake**: Reflexively offering $X/2. You've now anchored low AND signaled desperation.

### 20. Named Fake Objection
**What it is**: When the other person throws an objection you know is a test, name it.
**Why it works**: Shows you're not on autopilot. Sophisticated buyers respect it.
**Example line**: *"I hear you saying 'we already have a tool,' but my honest read is that's more a test than a no — is that fair?"*
**Common mistake**: Using it with an unsophisticated buyer. Feels rude.

### 21. Higher Authority
**What it is**: "I need to check with [partner / board / my manager]."
**Why it works**: Buys time, removes you from pressure, allows you to come back stronger.
**Example line**: *"Let me run this by my co-founder — I want to give you a real answer, not a quick one."*
**Common mistake**: Using it repeatedly — signals you have no authority.

### 22. Permission to Be Direct
**What it is**: Ask before saying something hard.
**Why it works**: Frames directness as a gift, not an attack.
**Example line**: *"Can I be blunt with you for a second?"*
**Common mistake**: Using it and then NOT being blunt. Breaks trust.

## 🟪 DISCOVERY & SELLING

### 23. SPIN (Situation → Problem → Implication → Need-payoff)
**What it is**: The 4-question arc of discovery selling.
**Why it works**: Builds the prospect's own case for change, in their own words.
**Example flow**:
  - S: *"How are you currently handling X?"*
  - P: *"Where does that break down?"*
  - I: *"What does that mean for your team / revenue / stress?"*
  - NP: *"If we could fix that, what would it be worth?"*
**Common mistake**: Skipping I (Implication). That's where the budget gets unlocked.

### 24. Teach–Tailor–Take-Control (Challenger)
**What it is**: Teach the prospect something new about their business (not about your product). Tailor it to their world. Then take control of the close.
**Why it works**: Prospects buy from people who taught them something, not people who asked what they want.
**Example line**: *"Most operators like you are losing 12-18% of inbound leads to response time — and the fix isn't hiring."*
**Common mistake**: Teaching something they already know.

### 25. Callback
**What it is**: Reference something they said earlier in the conversation.
**Why it works**: Proves you listened. Rare enough that it feels intimate.
**Example line** (date): *"You mentioned your favorite book earlier — did you ever read [her author's other book]?"*
**Example line** (sales): *"You said earlier that your team is burning out. If that's still true, here's the frame I'd suggest."*
**Common mistake**: Callback to the wrong thing, or to something throwaway. Proves you were scanning, not listening.

## 🟫 PRESS & PUBLIC STAKES

### 26. Don't Repeat the Wrong Number
**What it is**: If a reporter (or hostile counterpart) states a wrong fact, NEVER repeat it to correct it.
**Why it works**: Media quote the repeated number, not the correction. You own the wrong number forever.
**Example**: Reporter: *"So 3,000 users were affected?"* You: *"Let me give you the actual number — it was 842 users, between 2:04 and 2:45 AM."*
**Common mistake**: *"No, not 3,000..."* — congrats, they quote "3,000" in the article.

### 27. Concrete Timeline
**What it is**: In a crisis, give specific times, counts, actions.
**Why it works**: Vagueness = hiding. Specificity = accountability + control.
**Example line**: *"At 2:04 AM a deploy introduced a bug. At 2:45 AM our CTO patched it. By 9:00 AM, every affected user had a personalized email."*
**Common mistake**: "A short time" / "a small number" — screams spin.

### 28. Acknowledge → Reframe → Answer → Bridge
**What it is**: The 4-step formula for tough Q&A (interviews, pitches, press).
**Why it works**: Shows you heard, shows you're honest, controls where the answer goes.
**Example**: Judge: *"Why is this a billion-dollar market?"*
  - Acknowledge: *"Fair question — most decks overclaim on TAM."*
  - Reframe: *"Let me show you how we sized it honestly."*
  - Answer: *"Bottom-up: 8,000 qualifying companies × $12k ACV = $96M SAM today, growing 40%."*
  - Bridge: *"And that's why our focus is on the 400 who use [adjacent tool] — that's where our ICP concentrates."*
**Common mistake**: Skipping Acknowledge. Feels defensive.

## 🟩 DATING-SPECIFIC (still persuasion)

### 29. Vulnerability-Without-Neediness
**What it is**: Share something real about yourself without asking for reassurance.
**Why it works**: Creates intimacy. Signals security (which is attractive). Opposite of oversharing.
**Example line**: *"Honestly my last week's been a mess — three meetings I wasn't ready for. Tonight's the best part of it so far."*
**Common mistake**: Following it with "…but I don't want to bring the mood down" — kills the intimacy.

### 30. Push-Pull
**What it is**: Compliment, then a small tease. Or tease, then compliment.
**Why it works**: Keeps dynamic, avoids the "nice guy" trap.
**Example line**: *"You're way too smart to be this bad at picking restaurants."*
**Common mistake**: Too much push, not enough pull — feels mean.
